Biography

My research asks how microscopic interfaces and defects become measurable electronic-device behavior. I began with molecular contacts, self-assembled monolayers, and thin-film electrodes in organic and hybrid optoelectronics, then expanded toward defect-controlled transport, optoelectronic memory, and analog switching.

My current doctoral work examines how ionic dynamics, state-formation processes, and low-frequency noise limit multilevel memory precision and reliability. Printed-interconnect research adds a complementary foundation in process-induced variation and mechanical reliability; oxide electronics and integrated device platforms are future directions I aim to develop from this experimental base.
