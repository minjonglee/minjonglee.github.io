Biography

I began in organic and hybrid optoelectronics, studying molecular contacts, thin-film electrodes, and charge transport in photovoltaic and capacitor devices. That work led to first-author studies of interface-controlled optoelectronic memory and hydrogen-bond-engineered artificial synapses.

I now investigate how defects and ionic dynamics shape memory states, variability, and reliability. My doctoral project examines the precision limits of multilevel ionic memory; longer term, I aim to connect device physics with low-temperature integration and computing hardware. Government and industry programs in flexible interconnects and thin films broaden this materials-to-device perspective.
