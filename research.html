Core science

Interfaces, defects, ions & transport

How do molecular-scale interfaces and defects reshape electronic and ionic energy landscapes?

An electronic device is often governed by a small fraction of its material: the interface, the defect, or the pathway an ion can access. Understanding these local environments offers a route from chemical structure to device function.

Established work. Published work spans SAM-based hole-selective contacts, intermediate-layer-assisted trap reduction, and hydrogen-bond-driven artificial synapses.

Current question. Current work investigates state-formation dynamics and low-frequency noise to study the precision limits of ion-migration-controlled multilevel memristors. This is an active research question, with outcomes still to be established.

Methods. Self-assembled monolayers · Thin-film interface engineering · Electrical characterization · Charge-transport / trap analysis

Emerging question. Connect metastable states, stochastic switching, and electrical noise to a more predictive description of ionic memory. RTN-specific analysis and atomistic modeling remain directions to develop.

Device platform

Memory & reliability

How can interfacial and defect physics be translated into reliable—or deliberately stochastic—memory behavior?

Variability can limit precision, while controlled state dynamics may offer useful functionality. The challenge is to understand when disorder should be suppressed and when it can be used.

Published work. First-author studies address linear and symmetric artificial synapses and low-power optoelectronic memristors. Collaborative work also investigates the ionic landscape in perovskite memristors.

Current focus. My current focus includes low-power switching, variability, and reliability in memristors. DRAM MIM capacitors, dielectric interfaces, leakage, and stack integration are additional research interests identified in my CV.

Methods. I–V / C–V measurements · Pulse testing · Interface / electrode engineering · Device nonideality analysis

Emerging question. Explore retention–endurance–variability trade-offs and the boundary between binary and multilevel operation. Ferroelectric and memcapacitive concepts are future interests, not completed device platforms.

Device platform

Optoelectronics & hybrid devices

How do contacts and traps shape light conversion and detection?

Organic photovoltaics, photodetectors, transparent electrodes, and charge-selective molecular interfaces are a foundation of my research. They provide a connected platform for studying how contacts and traps shape transport, with future possibilities in optoelectronic memory and sensory hardware.

Established work. First-author papers address molecular hole-selective contacts and ALD oxide electrodes; collaborative publications span photodetectors and hybrid materials.

Methods and platforms. Self-assembled monolayers · charge-selective interfaces · organic photovoltaics · photodetectors · transparent electrodes.

Emerging question. How can these interface principles transfer to multifunctional optoelectronic memory?

Emerging direction

Integrated & 3D electronic systems

How can emerging memory and low-temperature electronic devices be integrated into scalable computing hardware?

Established foundation. Established work includes ALD-processed In₂O₃:V₂O₅ laminated electrodes for photovoltaic and capacitor devices. Thin-film process integration and electrical measurements provide a foundation for examining device nonidealities.

Future research. Low-temperature oxide semiconductors, BEOL-compatible devices, ferroelectrics, monolithic 3D integration, memory–logic integration, and compute-in-memory are emerging directions. Circuit- and system-level co-design is the intended next step.

Integrated and 3D systems are research directions, not completed device or circuit demonstrations in the supplied CV.
