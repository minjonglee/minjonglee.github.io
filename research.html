Molecular interfaces, defects & ionic dynamics

Interfacial & Nonequilibrium Physics

Established expertise + current research

How do molecular-scale interfaces and defects reshape electronic and ionic energy landscapes?

An electronic device is often governed by a small fraction of its material: the interface, the defect, or the pathway an ion can access. Understanding these local environments offers a route from chemical structure to device function.

Current research

Current work investigates state-formation dynamics and low-frequency noise to study the precision limits of ion-migration-controlled multilevel memristors. This is an active research question, with outcomes still to be established.

Established work / selected results

Published work spans SAM-based hole-selective contacts, intermediate-layer-assisted trap reduction, and hydrogen-bond-driven artificial synapses.

Methods & capabilities

  • Self-assembled monolayers
  • Thin-film interface engineering
  • Electrical characterization
  • Charge-transport / trap analysis

Emerging / future directions

Connect metastable states, stochastic switching, and electrical noise to a more predictive description of ionic memory. RTN-specific analysis and atomistic modeling remain directions to develop.

02

Resistive, capacitive & emerging memory

Adaptive & Reliable Memory Devices

Established expertise + current research

How can interfacial and defect physics be translated into reliable—or deliberately stochastic—memory behavior?

Variability can limit precision, while controlled state dynamics may offer useful functionality. The challenge is to understand when disorder should be suppressed and when it can be used.

Current research

My current focus includes low-power switching, variability, and reliability in memristors. DRAM MIM capacitors, dielectric interfaces, leakage, and stack integration are additional research interests identified in my CV.

Established work / selected results

First-author studies address linear and symmetric artificial synapses and low-power optoelectronic memristors. Collaborative work also investigates the ionic landscape in perovskite memristors.

Methods & capabilities

  • I–V / C–V measurements
  • Pulse testing
  • Interface / electrode engineering
  • Device nonideality analysis

Emerging / future directions

Explore retention–endurance–variability trade-offs and the boundary between binary and multilevel operation. Ferroelectric and memcapacitive concepts are future interests, not completed device platforms.

03

Oxide electronics, 3D integration & hardware-aware computing

Integrated Memory & Computing

Established foundations → emerging directions

How can emerging memory and low-temperature electronic devices be integrated into scalable computing hardware?

A useful device must eventually operate within the thermal, electrical, and variability constraints of a larger system. Linking these constraints back to materials choices is a long-term research objective.

Current research

Established work includes ALD-processed In₂O₃:V₂O₅ laminated electrodes for photovoltaic and capacitor devices. Thin-film process integration and electrical measurements provide a foundation for examining device nonidealities.

Established work / selected results

The 2023 first-author electrode study demonstrates a shared oxide platform for two device classes. CV-listed industry projects provide experience with ALD-based electrode development.

Methods & capabilities

  • Atomic layer deposition
  • Thin-film device processing
  • I–V / C–V / pulse testing
  • Physics-guided device analysis

Emerging / future directions

Low-temperature oxide semiconductors, BEOL-compatible devices, ferroelectrics, monolithic 3D integration, memory–logic integration, and compute-in-memory are emerging directions. Circuit- and system-level co-design is the intended next step.

Connected research

Organic & Hybrid Optoelectronics

Organic photovoltaics, photodetectors, transparent electrodes, and charge-selective molecular interfaces are a foundation of my research. They provide a connected platform for studying how contacts and traps shape transport, with future possibilities in optoelectronic memory and sensory hardware.

This background developed within AEEL at Korea University. Charge-selective interfaces and transparent electrodes link optoelectronic function to the same transport questions that now motivate my memory research.
