Research

From interfaces to integrated electronic systems.

I study how local structure, defects, mobile ions, and charge transport affect electronic-device behavior across several platforms.

Core physics

Interfaces · Defects · Ions · Transport

How do molecular-scale interfaces and defects reshape electronic and ionic energy landscapes?

An electronic device is often governed by a small fraction of its material: the interface, the defect, or the pathway an ion can access. Understanding these local environments offers a route from chemical structure to device function.

Published foundation. Published work spans SAM-based hole-selective contacts, intermediate-layer-assisted trap reduction, and hydrogen-bond-driven artificial synapses.

Methods. Self-assembled monolayers · Thin-film interface engineering · Electrical characterization · Charge-transport / trap analysis

Device platform

Memory & reliability

How can interfacial and defect physics be translated into reliable—or deliberately stochastic—memory behavior?

Variability can limit precision, while controlled state dynamics may offer useful functionality. The challenge is to understand when disorder should be suppressed and when it can be used.

Published work. First-author studies address linear and symmetric artificial synapses and low-power optoelectronic memristors. Collaborative work also investigates the ionic landscape in perovskite memristors.

Current research. My current focus includes low-power switching, variability, and reliability in memristors. DRAM MIM capacitors, dielectric interfaces, leakage, and stack integration are additional research interests identified in my CV.

Methods. I–V / C–V measurements · Pulse testing · Interface / electrode engineering · Device nonideality analysis

Device platform

Optoelectronics & hybrid devices

How do contacts and traps shape light conversion and detection?

Organic and hybrid devices provide a continuing platform for examining molecular contacts, thin-film electrodes, and charge-selective interfaces.

Published work. First-author papers address self-assembled contacts and ALD oxide electrodes; collaborative work includes photodetectors and hybrid materials.

Methods. Self-assembled monolayers · thin-film interfaces · organic photovoltaics · photodetectors.

Device platform · CV-listed programs

Flexible & stretchable electronics

How can electronic materials and interconnects remain useful under bending and stretching?

The supplied CV lists programs on highly flexible FPCB interconnect materials, stretchable PCBs using liquid metal particles, and durable stretchable adhesive films.

These programs bring mechanical deformation into the same materials-to-device questions of transport and reliability.

Emerging direction · future research

Integrated & 3D electronic systems

How can emerging memory and low-temperature electronic devices be integrated into scalable computing hardware?

Existing foundation. Established work includes ALD-processed In₂O₃:V₂O₅ laminated electrodes for photovoltaic and capacitor devices. Thin-film process integration and electrical measurements provide a foundation for examining device nonidealities.

Future research. Low-temperature oxide electronics, heterogeneous integration, and memory–logic systems are directions to develop from this device foundation.
