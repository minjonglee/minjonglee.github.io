Research

Device Physics and Reliability Across Electronic Platforms.

I investigate how interfaces, defects, and electronic and ionic transport govern device operation, variability, and reliability. My research connects physical mechanisms with experimental devices, extending from material and interface engineering to memory arrays, computing hardware, and emerging electronic platforms.

Research Areas

Core research

Memory Device Physics & Reliability

My core research examines how ionic and defect dynamics govern resistive and analog memory states. I study switching precision, noise, variability, retention, and endurance to connect device physics with reliability.

Neuromorphic Computing & Memory Circuits

I connect analog device behavior with crossbar arrays, synaptic learning, and CNN inference. Mixed-signal readout, ADC/FPGA-assisted evaluation, and vector–matrix computation extend that work toward memory circuits.

Semiconductor Interfaces & Oxide Electronics

My work includes molecular and thin-film interfaces, ALD-based electrodes, oxide electronics, and capacitor structures. I examine charge injection and leakage behavior as foundations for broader semiconductor reliability questions.

Flexible, Stretchable & Optoelectronic Devices

I study printed and stretchable interconnects, including electrical variability under deformation. Work on photodetectors and photovoltaic interfaces broadens this device-physics perspective across materials and operating conditions.

Research Outlook

From Device Physics to Reliable Electronic Systems.

I aim to carry insights from interface physics and device experiments into reliable memory and integrated electronic systems.

Future research directions

Predictive Memory Reliability

Building on ionic memory and interface-controlled transport, I aim to study how defects, noise, leakage, and stress shape memory failure. Future interests include oxide electronics and DRAM-relevant dielectric and capacitor interfaces.

Hardware-Aware Memory Computing

My work on analog memory, crossbar arrays, and circuit-level evaluation motivates variability-aware hardware and peripheral readout. I am interested in future memory–CMOS co-design.

Integrated & Adaptive Electronic Systems

Thin-film, optoelectronic, and flexible devices provide a foundation for exploring new architectures. Longer-term interests include low-temperature processing, heterogeneous systems, and three-dimensional integration.
