Research

Device Physics and Reliability Across Electronic Platforms.

I investigate how interfaces, defects, and electronic and ionic transport govern device operation, variability, and reliability. My research connects physical mechanisms with experimental devices, extending from material and interface engineering to memory arrays, computing hardware, and emerging electronic platforms.

Research Areas

Research Areas

Core research

Memory Device Physics & Reliability

My core research examines the physical mechanisms governing memory-state formation, resistive switching, and long-term stability. I investigate ionic and defect dynamics, stochastic transport, analog-state precision, low-frequency noise, retention, endurance, and device variability.

Neuromorphic Computing & Memory Circuits

I study how the electrical characteristics of memory devices translate into functional computing hardware. My research experience includes artificial synapses, crossbar arrays, learning behavior, CNN-based inference, mixed-signal readout, and vector–matrix computation.

Semiconductor Interfaces & Oxide Electronics

My work includes molecular and thin-film interface engineering, ALD-based electrode and oxide platforms, capacitor structures, charge injection, and leakage-related device behavior. These studies provide a foundation for investigating broader semiconductor interface and reliability challenges.

Connected device platforms

Flexible, Stretchable & Optoelectronic Devices

My research also spans printed and stretchable interconnects, deformation-induced electrical variability, photodetectors, photovoltaics, and hybrid electronic platforms. These studies extend my device-physics experience across different material systems, fabrication approaches, and operating conditions.

Research Outlook

From Device Physics to Reliable Electronic Systems.

I aim to translate fundamental understanding of interfaces, defects, and transport into reliable electronic technologies. Rather than focusing on a single material system, I seek to address transferable physical questions across semiconductor memories, computing hardware, and integrated electronic platforms.

Future research directions

Predictive Memory Reliability

Building on my research in ionic memory and interface-controlled transport, I aim to investigate how defects, electrical noise, leakage, and operating stress determine memory precision, variability, and failure. Future interests include oxide-based electronics and DRAM-relevant dielectric and capacitor interfaces.

Hardware-Aware Memory Computing

My experience with analog memory, crossbar arrays, and circuit-level evaluation motivates further research connecting device nonidealities with reliable computation. I am particularly interested in variability-aware hardware design, peripheral readout, and future memory–CMOS co-design.

Integrated & Adaptive Electronic Systems

My research experience in thin films, optoelectronics, and flexible electronics provides a foundation for exploring new electronic architectures. Longer-term interests include low-temperature device integration, heterogeneous electronic systems, and emerging three-dimensional platforms.
