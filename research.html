Research

Interfaces, defects, and transport across electronic devices.

I study how microscopic interfaces and electronic or ionic transport govern device operation, variability, and reliability. Three research pillars connect physical questions to memory devices and thin-film electronic platforms.

Research pillar 01

Interface & Defect Engineering

Research question

How do molecular interfaces and defects modify charge transport and device behavior?

What I control
Molecular contacts · self-assembled monolayers · intermediate-layer and electrode interfaces
What I measure
Charge injection and transport · interface energetics · trap-related device response
Why it matters
Connecting interfacial chemistry to measurable electrical behavior helps identify the origins of loss and instability.

Research pillar 03

Thin-Film Electronics & Device Platforms

Research question

How do thin-film interfaces, processing conditions, and material properties translate into electrical performance and reliability?

What I control
Thin-film electrode and contact design · printed-film thickness and trace geometry
What I measure
Device transport and leakage · initial trace-to-trace variability · electrical response under repeated deformation
Why it matters
Separating processing variation from operating stress clarifies which material and geometric changes matter in working devices.

Selected study

Molecular interfaces for reliable analog memory

Advanced Materials · 2025 · First author

Scientific question
Can hydrogen bonding at a polymer–perovskite interface improve the symmetry and stability of analog switching?
Physical mechanism
Interfacial hydrogen bonding suppresses trap-mediated disruption of ionic and electronic response.
Experimental strategy
Compare interface-controlled synaptic devices through switching and state-retention measurements.
Key evidence
More than eightfold lower interfacial trap density and 50 analog conductance states retained beyond 10⁴ s.
Scientific contribution
Interfacial chemistry offers a physical route to more reproducible analog memory states.

Selected study

Defect-controlled optoelectronic memory

Advanced Functional Materials · 2025 · First author

Scientific question
Can an intermediate layer lower trap density while preserving low-power optoelectronic switching?
Physical mechanism
An ALD TiO₂ intermediate layer changes the device interface and reduces trapping.
Experimental strategy
Relate intermediate-layer design to trap-density estimates and switching measurements.
Key evidence
A fifteenfold reduction in interfacial trap density, +0.24 V SET operation, and approximately 0.7 μW switching power.
Scientific contribution
The study connects a controlled interface to both the switching mechanism and its operating cost.

Intermediate Layer-Assisted Trap Density Reduction in Low-Power Optoelectronic Memristors for Multifunctional Systems · Paper / DOI

Selected study

Persistent analog states conditioned by optical helicity

Advanced Materials · 2026 · Co-first author

Scientific question
Can optical helicity condition distinguishable electronic memory states that remain readable over time?
Physical mechanism
Helicity-dependent excitation biases persistent conductance states in a chiral perovskite synapse.
Experimental strategy
Evaluate retained conductance, readout-noise-limited state capacity, and a small device array.
My role
Conceptualization, methodology, investigation, data curation, and original-draft writing, as listed in the publisher’s CRediT statement.
Key evidence
72 experimentally distinguishable states, 10⁶ s retention, read-noise analysis, and evaluation of a 7×7 device array.
Scientific contribution
The result tests whether an optical input can become a persistent analog state with measurable precision and array-level behavior.

Helicity-Defined Analog Conductance States in Chiral Perovskite Synapses · Paper / DOI
