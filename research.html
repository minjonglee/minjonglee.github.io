Research

Device Physics and Reliability Across Electronic Platforms.

I study how interfacial chemistry, defects, and electronic and ionic transport shape memory states, switching variation, and reliability. Published device and array studies test these mechanisms across several electronic platforms.

Research Areas

Core research

Memory Device Physics & Reliability

My core research examines how ionic and defect dynamics govern resistive and analog memory states. I study switching precision, noise, variability, retention, and endurance to connect device physics with reliability.

Core research

Neuromorphic Devices & Array-Level Computing

I connect analog conductance control with fabricated crossbar arrays and computing validation. Published studies include synaptic learning, image classification, PCB-integrated ADC/FPGA readout, and mixed-signal vector–matrix multiplication.

Semiconductor Interfaces & Oxide Electronics

My work includes molecular and thin-film interfaces, ALD-based electrodes, oxide electronics, and capacitor structures. I examine charge injection and leakage behavior as foundations for broader semiconductor reliability questions.

Flexible, Stretchable & Optoelectronic Devices

I study printed and stretchable interconnects, including electrical variability under deformation. Work on photodetectors and photovoltaic interfaces broadens this device-physics perspective across materials and operating conditions.

Published research · Advanced Materials (2025)

Hydrogen-Bond-Engineered Analog Memory

Can hydrogen-bonded interfaces reduce trap-driven variation in analog synaptic updates?

Physical mechanism
PVA hydroxyl groups bond with surface iodides in CsPbI₃, reducing interfacial traps and stabilizing ion transport.
Experimental evidence
DFT and electrical analysis; fabricated 7 × 7 crossbar; image-classification evaluation.
Key achievement
>8× lower interfacial trap density · ≈12.5 pJ/update · >10⁴ s retention at 85 °C.
My contribution
First author of the published study.

Published research · Advanced Materials (2026)

Helicity-Defined Persistent Analog Memory

Can optical helicity program durable analog states that remain electrically distinguishable in an addressed array?

Physical mechanism
Chirality-matched circularly polarized light conditions persistent conductance updates in layered chiral perovskite synapses.
Experimental evidence
State-resolved retention and read-noise analysis; array conductance maps and mixed-signal vector–matrix multiplication.
Key achievement
72 statistically distinguishable states through 10⁶ s · 7 × 7 array · PCB-integrated readout.
My contribution
Co-first author; the publisher credits conceptualization, methodology, investigation, data curation, and original-draft writing.

Published research · Advanced Functional Materials (2025)

Trap-Engineered Optoelectronic Memristors

How can an intermediate layer improve low-power optoelectronic memory?

Physical mechanism
An ALD TiO₂ nanofilm passivates perovskite-interface defects and improves electron-transport alignment.
Experimental evidence
Electrical and optical device characterization; 5 × 5 array image-recognition evaluation.
Key achievement
15× lower interfacial trap density · +0.24 V SET · ≈0.7 µW switching power.
My contribution
First author of the published study.

Published research · 2023–2024

Molecular and ALD-Based Interface Engineering

How do molecular contacts and ALD-grown electrodes change charge extraction across device platforms?

Physical mechanism
SAM dipoles tune electrode work function and hole extraction; a separate ALD study develops laminated In₂O₃:V₂O₅ electrode stacks.
Experimental evidence
Published device studies of SAM-treated contacts and ALD-processed laminated electrodes.
Key achievement
Two first-author papers connect interface design with photovoltaic and capacitor operation.
My contribution
First author on both published papers.

Accepted research · 2026

Printed Interconnects and Electrical Reliability

How much of the electrical variation between nominally identical stretchable traces originates in printed-film thickness?

Physical mechanism
Printed-film thickness contributes to initial trace-to-trace resistance variation; deformation-related changes are analyzed separately.
Experimental evidence
Accepted manuscript record on thickness and trace-to-trace electrical variability; related stretchable-PCB project.
Key achievement
Distinguishes process-origin electrical variation from resistance changes during repeated deformation.
My contribution
First author on the accepted manuscript; participating researcher in the related industry project.

Research Outlook

From Device Physics to Reliable Electronic Systems.

These questions extend published device and array results toward predictive reliability and integrated electronics.

Future research directions

Predictive Memory Reliability

Can noise signatures and stochastic state evolution identify the physical sources of analog memory variability and predict the onset of reliability degradation?

Hardware-Aware Memory Computing

How do device variability, read noise, and nonlinear updates propagate into array-computation errors? I aim to test mitigation through device and peripheral design; memory–CMOS co-design remains a future interest.

Integrated & Adaptive Electronic Systems

How do interfacial defects, charge-injection barriers, and processing conditions determine leakage and long-term stability in thin-film, dielectric, and capacitor structures? Heterogeneous and 3D integration remain longer-term interests.
