MIN JONG LEE

Interface & device physics
for emerging electronics.

I investigate how molecular interfaces, defects, and ionic dynamics shape charge transport and persistent electronic states. My work connects perovskite memory, neuromorphic devices, and optoelectronics with the question of how emerging devices can operate reliably.

Advanced Materials · 2026Co-first author

Helicity-Defined Analog Conductance States in Chiral Perovskite Synapses

Optical helicity biases persistent analog conductance states in chiral perovskite synapses; read-noise analysis connects state capacity to device reliability.

72 experimentally distinct states · 10⁶ s retention · 7×7 device array

Paper / DOI Featured by Korea University

Advanced Materials · 2025

Linear and Symmetric Artificial Synapses Driven by Hydrogen Bonding for Accurate and Reliable Neuromorphic Computing

Hydrogen bonding at the polymer–perovskite interface suppresses traps and stabilizes analog switching, improving the linearity needed for neuromorphic computation.

>8× lower interfacial trap density · 50 analog states retained for >10⁴ s

Paper / DOI

Advanced Functional Materials · 2025

Intermediate Layer-Assisted Trap Density Reduction in Low-Power Optoelectronic Memristors for Multifunctional Systems

An ALD TiO₂ intermediate layer limits interfacial trapping and enables low-voltage switching in optoelectronic memristors, linking interface design to device function.

15× lower interfacial trap density · +0.24 V SET · ≈0.7 μW switching power
